### [Study identifies five engagement phenotypes among 102,684 Ash chatbot users](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-78802-w) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A peer-reviewed observational study of 102,684 users of the AI mental health chatbot Ash identified five engagement phenotypes by clustering eight behavioral features: Early Dropouts \(52.2%\), Weekly Users \(25.3%\), Concentrated Users \(16.8%\), Intensive Users \(4.1%\), and Power Users \(1.6%\). Among users completing baseline and three-week measures \(n=298 for PHQ-9 and GAD-7; n=194 for social support\), the study reported pre-post improvements in depression \(d=-0.51\), anxiety \(d=-0.57\), and social support \(d=0.22\), with the depression dose-response gradient replicated in a model-predicted PHQ-9 sample of 23,813. The largest depression improvements were among Power and Intensive Users \(d=-0.57 and -0.50\) and the smallest among Early Dropouts \(d=-0.22\); higher baseline working alliance \(n=11,437\) predicted depression improvement and moderated the engagement-social-support relationship. The authors describe the evidence as naturalistic and correlational, note the small clinical subsamples, and warn against treating session counts as a primary engagement metric; four authors are employees of the company building the model, and Slingshot AI funded one author&\#x27;s research.

rss · Nature Psychology · Nov 15, 00:00

**「Background」** AI mental health chatbots are often promoted as scalable alternatives or complements to clinician-led care, yet engagement is commonly summarized by session counts, which do not capture the timing, intensity, or consistency of use. This study instead clusters users on eight behavioral features and pairs those patterns with standard symptom and working-alliance measures.

**「Impact」** For developers and researchers, the result argues against session counts as a stand-alone engagement metric and supports measuring the timing and pattern of use separately when relating engagement to clinical outcomes. Because the symptom comparisons are observational, they support further controlled testing rather than claims that the chatbot itself caused the improvements.
