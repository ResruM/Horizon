### [Ash study finds five engagement phenotypes, symptom associations](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-78802-w) ⭐️ 7.0/10

An observational analysis of 102,684 users of the purpose-built AI mental health chatbot Ash identified five engagement phenotypes—Early Dropouts \(52.2%\), Weekly Users \(25.3%\), Concentrated Users \(16.8%\), Intensive Users \(4.1%\), and Power Users \(1.6%\)—and found that 66.9% had at least one session between 9 p.m. and 5 a.m. In small clinical subsamples assessed at baseline and three weeks, mean improvements were d = -0.51 on the PHQ-9 \(n = 298\), d = -0.57 on the GAD-7 \(n = 298\), and d = 0.22 on the MSPSS social-support scale \(n = 194\); a dose-response gradient showed the largest depression improvements among Power Users \(d = -0.57\) and the smallest among Early Dropouts \(d = -0.22\). The authors report that the gradient was replicated in a larger sample using model-predicted PHQ-9 scores \(n = 23,813\), and that higher baseline working alliance \(11,437 users\) predicted depression improvement and moderated the engagement–social-support relationship. They caution that session counts are a poor primary engagement metric and describe the evidence as correlational, pending controlled designs; four authors are employees of the company building the model.

rss · Nature Psychology · Nov 15, 00:00

**「Background」** Ash is a consumer AI mental health chatbot from Slingshot AI, a lab building a foundation model for psychology; the company positions Ash as a self-help aid for stress, anxiety, and relationships rather than a diagnostic or treatment tool \(tool-2-1, tool-2-3\). The study&\#x27;s authors include Slingshot employees, so the analysis combines company-built technology with clinical measures.

**「Impact」** Developers and clinical evaluators should not use raw session counts as the primary engagement metric: the study found five distinct use patterns, and higher baseline working alliance \(11,437 users\) predicted depression improvement and moderated the engagement–social-support relationship.
