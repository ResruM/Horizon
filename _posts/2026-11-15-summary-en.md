### [Study maps engagement patterns among 102,684 Ash chatbot users](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-78802-w) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A study in Scientific Reports used k-means clustering on eight behavioral features to identify five engagement phenotypes among 102,684 users of Ash, a purpose-built AI mental health chatbot: Early Dropouts \(52.2%\), Weekly Users \(25.3%\), a novel Concentrated User pattern \(16.8%\), Intensive Users \(4.1%\), and Power Users \(1.6%\). Among users, 66.9% had at least one overnight session between 9 p.m. and 5 a.m. In follow-up subsamples assessed at baseline and three weeks, pre-post improvements were reported for depression \(PHQ-9, n=298, d=-0.51\), anxiety \(GAD-7, n=298, d=-0.57\), and social support \(MSPSS, n=194, d=0.22\); a dose-response gradient in depression improvement was replicated in a larger model-predicted PHQ-9 sample \(n=23,813\), with the largest improvements among Power Users \(d=-0.57\) and Intensive Users \(d=-0.50\) and the smallest among Early Dropouts \(d=-0.22\). The authors describe the evidence as naturalistic and correlational, report that 11,437 users completed the baseline Working Alliance Inventory, caution against treating session counts as a primary engagement metric, and note that four authors are employees of the company building the model.

rss · Nature Psychology · Nov 15, 00:00

**「Background」** Ash is a conversational mental health app built by Slingshot AI, a research lab developing a foundation model for psychology; the company describes Ash as an AI designed for mental health support. A June 2026 review listed the app as free to download and use, with a subscription model planned.

**「Impact」** For teams evaluating AI mental health chatbots, the findings support measuring distinct engagement phenotypes and working alliance rather than relying on raw session counts alone; higher working alliance predicted depression improvement and moderated the engagement–social support relationship. Because the clinical follow-up subsamples were small and the design was observational, these associations should not be presented as evidence that the chatbot caused the improvements.

### [Small trial reports motivation and GPA gains from AI literacy framework](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-79820-4) ⭐️ 5.0/10

A two-group pre-test–post-test study of 80 undergraduates at one institution reports that students who received instruction supported by the Motivation–Performance Adaptive AI Literacy Optimization Framework \(MP-AILOF\) showed statistically significant increases in academic self-efficacy, learning motivation, and GPA, while a 40-student control group received conventional classroom instruction. Analysis of covariance adjusting for baseline scores found higher adjusted post-test scores in the experimental group across the three outcomes. The authors caution that the sample is small and single-institution, that GPA values need verification against the institutional grading scale, and that the supplied implementation package does not independently validate the framework’s proposed GBR prediction and adaptive optimization components.

rss · Nature Psychology · Nov 15, 00:00

**「Background」** The framework’s premise is that access to AI tools alone is insufficient and that students’ motivation, academic self-efficacy, and learning progress also matter for personalized learning. The study used a pre-test–post-test design with ANCOVA to compare post-intervention outcomes after adjusting for baseline scores, a design that can show associations with the intervention but does not by itself establish causality or generalizability.

**「Impact」** Instructors and administrators considering AI literacy pilots have a concrete model to test—combining AI instruction with motivational assessment and performance monitoring—but the single-institution, 80-student evidence and unverified GPA records mean it should be treated as a preliminary signal rather than a validated program.
