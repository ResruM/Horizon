### [Study maps five engagement patterns among 102,684 AI mental health chatbot users](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-78802-w) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A peer-reviewed observational study of 102,684 users of the AI mental health chatbot Ash used k-means clustering on eight behavioral features to identify five engagement phenotypes: Early Dropouts \(52.2%\), Weekly Users \(25.3%\), a newly described Concentrated User pattern \(16.8%\), Intensive Users \(4.1%\), and Power Users \(1.6%\). In subsamples assessed at baseline and three weeks, PHQ-9 depression scores improved \(d = −0.51, n = 298\), GAD-7 anxiety scores improved \(d = −0.57, n = 298\), and MSPSS social support improved \(d = 0.22, n = 194\). A depression dose-response gradient was replicated in a larger model-predicted PHQ-9 sample \(n = 23,813\), with the largest improvements among Power Users \(d = −0.57\) and Intensive Users \(d = −0.50\) and the smallest among Early Dropouts \(d = −0.22\); higher baseline working alliance \(n = 11,437\) predicted depression improvement. The authors describe the evidence as naturalistic and correlational, caution against treating session counts as a primary engagement metric, and say controlled designs are needed to confirm clinical benefit.

rss · Nature Psychology · Nov 15, 00:00

**「Background」** Ash is a purpose-built mental health chatbot from Slingshot AI, which describes itself as a mental health research lab building a foundation model for psychology rather than a general-purpose assistant \[tool-2-1\]\[tool-2-2\]. The company markets Ash as a private, zero-judgment space for stress, anxiety, relationships, and longer-term growth \[tool-2-2\].

**「Impact」** The results give developers and evaluators of AI mental health tools a reason to measure when and how consistently people use a product, not just how many sessions they complete: the study reports that different clinical outcomes respond to different dimensions of use and that working alliance predicted depression improvement. That makes raw session counts a weak target for product optimization or for claims that a chatbot is clinically effective.
